How critical hours are defined

Critical hours drive the capacity value and most of the plan. Here is exactly what Pantheon means by them, what you can change, and how we score ourselves once CENACE publishes the year’s list, once, around February of the following year.

TO VERIFYThe rule below reflects the market as Pantheon understands it. The capacity balance market and how storage counts may change under the 2025 law; [CLIENT]'s counsel and [TRADER] confirm before the first statement is relied on.

The rule, as Pantheon uses it

Settings · critical hours
  1. CENACE decides after the fact, once a year. The 100 hours of highest system demand in the year are the critical hours for the capacity balance market. Nobody knows them in advance.
  2. Pantheon forecasts a probability that each hour of tomorrow lands in that set, from demand forecasts, the last 3 years' pattern, weather and the calendar. Hours at or above the threshold are shown as likely.
  3. The plan discharges in likely hours so the plant is seen delivering when it matters, unless a hard limit says otherwise.
  4. When CENACE publishes the year’s list (once, around February of the following year), Pantheon reconciles: which likely hours were real, which were false alarms, which real ones were missed. That score is on the Forecast page, and the capacity value moves from projected to verified.

Parameters

Changing these re-scores every forecast from today; past replays keep the rule they were scored with. Logged.
