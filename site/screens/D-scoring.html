How this is scored

How every forecast is scored

1
Freeze

Each day’s forecast for tomorrow is locked before the CENACE day-ahead deadline (10:00 plant time). The locked file includes 24 hourly prices, the likely range, and a critical-hour likelihood per hour.

2
Fingerprint

A SHA-256 fingerprint of the locked file is written to the audit log and published on the Forecast screen at the moment of freezing. The file can be downloaded by anyone with access; its fingerprint proves it was not changed after prices were known.

3
Wait for CENACE

When CENACE publishes the day-ahead (MDA) and real-time (MTR) prices for the node, and, once a year (around February), the list of the year’s critical hours, Pantheon scores the frozen file against them automatically. Scoring is never run by a person.

4
Price error

Mean absolute percentage error across the 24 hours: for each hour |forecast − actual| / actual, averaged. The daily figure on the Forecast screen is this number; the 30-day figure is the average of the daily figures. Target: 15%.

5
Critical hours

An hour counts as flagged when its likelihood was 50% or more in the frozen file. Critical hours are scored once a year, when CENACE publishes the year’s list (around February); until then they are counted as flagged, not scored. A hit is a flagged hour that is on the list. A miss is a listed hour that was not flagged. A false alarm is a flagged hour not on the list; false alarms are reported but do not reduce the hit count.

6
Peak timing

The day’s highest-priced hour in the frozen forecast, compared with the actual highest-priced hour. Within one hour counts as correct.

7
What is excluded

Days where CENACE did not publish final prices are shown as pending, not scored. Days with a feed outage on our side are scored anyway: a missing forecast is a miss for every critical hour that day.
